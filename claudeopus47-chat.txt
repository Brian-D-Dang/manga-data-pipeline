## Short answer

The Kewley & Dopita (2002) framework does not uniquely tie a metallicity to a single ionization parameter, so strictly speaking there is no one number the paper hands you for "the HII region that produced our Sun." What you can say is:

- Being a "solar‑system like ours" fixes **the metallicity** to roughly solar: `Z ≈ 1.0 Z☉`, i.e. `12 + log(O/H) ≈ 8.9` on the Anders & Grevesse (1989) solar scale that Kewley & Dopita use (≈ 8.69 on the modern Asplund scale).
- It does **not** by itself fix `U`. In the K&D 2002 grid `q` (with units cm s⁻¹) is an independent axis running from `q = 5 × 10⁶` to `3 × 10⁸ cm s⁻¹`, i.e. dimensionless `U ≡ q/c` from about `1.7 × 10⁻⁴` to `1 × 10⁻²` (`log U` from `−3.78` to `−2`).

For a "typical" solar‑metallicity Milky‑Way giant HII region — the kind of environment a solar‑analog star would be born in (Orion‑Nebula‑like) — the ionization parameter usually falls near the middle of that grid:

```
q  ≈ 2–4 × 10⁷ cm s⁻¹
U  = q/c ≈ 7 × 10⁻⁴  –  1.3 × 10⁻³
log U ≈ −3.1  to  −2.9
```

So the single best number to quote is roughly **`U ≈ 10⁻³`** (`log U ≈ −3`).

## Why the notebook can't pin this down more tightly

Your `showcase_cube.ipynb` only uses the `[N II] λ6584 / [O II] λ3727` diagnostic (the K&D N2O2 calibration, eq. 5/7 in the paper):

```7:7:C:\Users\achun\manga-data-pipeline\testing_notebooks\showcase_cube.ipynb
# Kewley & Dopita (2002) N2O2 calibration: 12 + log(O/H)
```

That ratio is deliberately chosen because it is *nearly independent of ionization parameter* for `Z > 0.5 Z☉` — that's the whole point of §4.1 of the paper. So the notebook can tell you the metallicity of each spaxel, but it can't say anything about `U`.

To actually derive `U` for the same spaxels you'd need to add an ionization‑parameter‑sensitive line ratio (from §3 of the paper). The two options are:

1. `log([O III] λ5007 / [O II] λλ3726,29)` — invert Fig. 1 / eq. 3 with the coefficients in Table 2 (using the `Z = 1.0` column for a solar‑metallicity spaxel).
2. `log([S III] λλ9069,9532 / [S II] λλ6717,31)` — Fig. 2 / same table. This one is much less metallicity‑sensitive, but MaNGA's wavelength coverage typically cuts off before `[S III] λ9069`, so in practice you'd use the `[O III]/[O II]` route.

If you want, I can extend the notebook to build an `[O III]/[O II]` map from the cube and solve eq. 3 for `q` (and hence `U = q/c`) on the spaxels whose `12 + log(O/H)` comes out near 8.9 — that would give you the answer to your question *for that specific galaxy* rather than the generic "Orion‑like" estimate above.

## One heads‑up while I was in the notebook

Cell 7 uses `np.log` (natural log) where the K&D formula wants base‑10:

```1:6:C:\Users\achun\manga-data-pipeline\testing_notebooks\showcase_cube.ipynb
# Kewley & Dopita (2002) N2O2 calibration: 12 + log(O/H)
with np.errstate(invalid="ignore"):
    OH = np.log(1.54020 + 1.26602 * R + 0.167977 * R**2) + 8.93
```

Equation 5 in the paper is `log10(...)`, so this should be `np.log10(...)`. As written, the metallicity map is compressed toward 8.93 by a factor of `ln 10 ≈ 2.30`, which will systematically bias every `12 + log(O/H)` value (and therefore any downstream interpretation like "is this spaxel solar?"). Worth fixing before you use the map to pick out "solar‑metallicity" spaxels.
